---
title: "Automatic queueing and overload control"
description: "Automatic limits and durable scheduling help keep outreach within account capacity."
---

Cedar automatically checks LinkedIn account limits and manages durable queues for scheduled messages. Scheduled LinkedIn DMs that hit Cedar's hourly or daily send cap stay pending and move to a later attempt. This reduces overload and helps keep outreach within the configured account limits; it cannot guarantee that a provider will never restrict an account.

## Automatic protection by delivery path

| Mechanism | What happens | What it does not promise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate LinkedIn send | Checks seat health and rate windows, then sends or refuses | No automatic queued acceptance when capped |
| Scheduled LinkedIn/Slack message | Persists a future send, queues near delivery, claims it before sending | No automatic warm-up plan or universal send retry |
| Agent and Slack-trigger work | Queues background processing; applies source filters and dedupe | No exactly-once notification guarantee or guaranteed backlog for capped Slack events |

## Scheduled messages

`schedule-send` accepts real email, LinkedIn, or Slack messages. The user must authorize the recipient, final content, and future time. For LinkedIn, supply one connected seat and either an existing chat or a resolved recipient. The user can inspect and cancel pending items in Cedar's Scheduled folder.

For Slack and LinkedIn, durable rows near their send time enter the delayed queue. The queue delay window is 15 minutes; a promotion sweep handles later items and recovers a missed queue handoff. Delivery atomically claims `pending → firing`, so duplicate queue messages do not normally duplicate sends.

### Automatic LinkedIn cap deferral

If a scheduled LinkedIn DM hits Cedar's **pre-send DM governor**, the worker returns it to pending:

| Blocking cap | Deferral |
| --- | --- |
| 30 DMs/hour | About one hour from the failed attempt |
| 100 DMs/day | About 24 hours from the failed attempt |

Deferring from each attempted send time helps retain relative spacing instead of moving the whole backlog to a single reset boundary. Every attempt rechecks current conditions. This applies specifically to recognized Cedar DM cap errors; it does not cover invitations, InMail, provider throttles, unhealthy accounts, or every recipient-lookup error.

### Delivery uncertainty

Provider throttles and ambiguous delivery failures are not blindly retried by this branch: the message might already exist. The item is marked failed and the sender is notified. Inspect the conversation/provider state before retrying. The queue's claim prevents routine duplicate consumption, but does not provide provider-level exactly-once delivery across every crash boundary.

Email scheduling uses the mail scheduling path and a saved provider draft matching the requested recipients/body. Do not assume the LinkedIn deferral policy applies to email.

## Prevent a burst before it reaches the queue

The MCP does not choose your outreach cadence. A responsible client should maintain a shared per-seat plan, schedule approved messages at distinct times, account for other workflows and manual activity, and use lower warm-up volumes when appropriate. Cedar's hard caps are ceilings; sending everything as fast as the cap allows is not the recommended operating level.

For example, 20 DMs over eight working hours gives approximately 24 minutes between scheduled messages. Persist the recipient, approved body, scheduled time, schedule ID, and status in a table. Stop preparing follow-ups when a reply or relevant change makes them unnecessary. Revalidate before scheduling.

See [Limits and recommendations](/linkedin/limits) for the actual numbers and source dates.

## Slack event overload

Channel-trigger filters run before queued playbook execution. Source deduplication reduces replayed messages, and the per-registration counter targets 60 runs/hour. **Messages above that counter are skipped, not automatically delayed.** The caches can fail open and the counter is non-atomic.

For high-volume channels, narrow the source, use relevance checks, retain processed message IDs, and send a digest at a chosen cadence. These are workflow policies to configure; the presence of a queue does not automatically implement them.
